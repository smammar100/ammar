## The first investment is always the hardest

Not because people don't save. Because moving money into something unfamiliar requires confidence.

<Goals>
<Goal n="01" title="Make trust visible">
Show the compliance, screening and destination of their money.
</Goal>
<Goal n="02" title="Remove the friction">
Make onboarding as seamless as possible since we are totally digital.
</Goal>
<Goal n="03" title="Build from what they know">
We started with familiar products like Mutual Fund and Retirement and then slowly added more
</Goal>
</Goals>

## What that turned into

Three surfaces have to feel like one app: a retirement plan measured in decades, a market that moves every second, and an advisory layer that explains both to someone seeing it for the first time.

<MahaanaScreenWall />

<Feature title="One screen that answers “how am I doing?”">
Net asset value, contributions and active pots resolve into a single number, with the projection pushed out to twenty-five years. Risk reads as a plain three-way split, money market against debt against equity, instead of a score nobody can act on.
</Feature>

<Feature title="Products framed as goals, not funds">
Save+, the pension scheme and the gold vault each read as something you are saving *for*. Every card carries its compliance marker and expected yield, so the Shariah question is answered before anyone thinks to ask it.
</Feature>

<Feature title="A reason to open the app on a day you aren't depositing">
Index tickers, sector heatmaps, Mahaana's Pick, gainers and losers give the app a pulse between deposits. Market wrap, explainer videos and a prompt-led assistant catch the first-time investor at the moment the numbers stop being obvious.
</Feature>

## Holding it together

Four products across two platforms only stay one product if the pieces are shared. The system stays deliberately small: three brand colours and two type roles, composed into the cards, list rows and account picker that every journey reuses.

<MahaanaFoundations />

Building the libraries this way cut design-to-development handoff by 35%, mostly by ending the round-trip where engineering has to ask what a spacing value or a state is supposed to be.

## What I took from it

The hard part was never the interface. It was the translation: taking instruments that are genuinely complicated, and that carry a religious compliance requirement most fintech never has to think about, and making them legible without flattening them into a lie.

The design system is what made that survive two platforms. Every simplification had to hold up as a real component an engineer could build twice, which is a stricter test than anything a design file can tell you on its own.
